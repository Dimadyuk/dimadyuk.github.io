Privacy Policy

This privacy policy applies to the UV Index app (also published as UV Index Widget & SPF Forecast) for Android and iOS (Android package: app.weather.sun.uv.index), together with its Wear OS app, Apple Watch app, home-screen widgets and any related services operated by Dzmitry Dzemidziuk (collectively, the "Application"). Dzmitry Dzemidziuk is hereby referred to as the "Service Provider". This single policy covers both platforms and all companion apps; where a rule applies to only one of them, the platform is stated explicitly.


Summary

The Application shows the UV index and related sun-safety information for your location. It has no user accounts and requires no registration. The Service Provider operates no servers of its own and does not receive your location, your skin type, your sunscreen settings or your saved places. Your settings and saved places stay on your device.

What leaves your device is limited to: (1) your location coordinates, sent to the Open-Meteo weather service so it can return the UV and air-quality data for where you are; (2) location lookups handled by your platform's geocoder (Apple or Google) to name a place or find a city you search for; (3) usage and crash diagnostics collected by Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics; and (4) purchase information handled by the app store and RevenueCat when you subscribe or restore a purchase. The Application does not currently display advertising and does not use the advertising identifier. Everything is described below.


Data Stored On Your Device Only

The following stays on your device and is never transmitted to the Service Provider:

  • Profile settings — skin type, sunscreen (SPF) settings, vitamin D goals, units, theme, language and onboarding choices.
  • Saved locations — the places you add, with their coordinates and names, and the cached UV, forecast and air-quality data for them, stored in an on-device database.
  • Alert and timer settings — the times and thresholds you choose for UV alerts and the state of the SPF timer. Notifications are scheduled and shown locally on your device; the Application does not use push-notification servers.
  • Widget data — a snapshot of the latest UV data shared between the Application and its home-screen widgets (via shared on-device storage).
  • Watch data — if you use the Wear OS or Apple Watch app, the same snapshot (UV values, your chosen location name and its coordinates, and your skin type) is passed directly between your phone and your own watch through the platform's watch-connectivity service (Google Wearable Data Layer or Apple WatchConnectivity). It does not pass through the Service Provider.

Location

The Application asks for permission to access your device location so that it can show the UV index where you are. You can also choose a place by searching for it, without granting location access.

  • What is collected. On Android the Application may request precise (fine) or approximate (coarse) location, depending on what you allow. On iOS it requests location while the app is in use and uses precise or approximate location depending on your choice. The Application does not access your location in the background and does not track your movements.
  • How it is used. The coordinates are used only to retrieve the UV index, forecast and air-quality data for that place, to determine the local time zone, and to display the name of the place. When a home-screen widget or watch app refreshes, it uses the last location you chose in the Application.
  • Who receives it. The coordinates are sent over HTTPS to Open-Meteo (see below). The place name and time zone are resolved by Apple (CLGeocoder / MapKit) on iOS or by the Android system geocoder (which on most devices is provided by Google) on Android. When you search for a city, the text you type is sent to the same geocoder. The coordinates are not sent to Firebase Analytics.
  • Control. You can deny or revoke the permission at any time in your device settings; the Application then works with places you search for and add manually.

Weather and UV Data (Open-Meteo)

UV, forecast and air-quality data are provided by Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com), whose data is licensed under CC BY 4.0. Each request contains the latitude and longitude of the place you are viewing and standard network metadata such as your IP address, which the operator of the service may log. No account, advertising identifier, name or email address is sent. Weather-condition icons are loaded from openweathermap.org; that request reveals only your IP address and the icon requested.


Analytics and Crash Reporting

The Application uses Google Analytics for Firebase, Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Remote Config, provided by Google, to understand how features are used in aggregate, to detect and fix defects, and to run product experiments (for example which onboarding variant is shown). They are used for product improvement and are not used to build advertising profiles.

  • Firebase Analytics may process: a randomly generated app-instance identifier; the events you trigger inside the Application (opening a screen, using the SPF timer, viewing the paywall, turning alerts on, completing onboarding, and similar); event parameters that describe your choices, such as the skin type you select (Fitzpatrick type), your SPF and sun-exposure choices, your vitamin D goal and the goals you pick during onboarding, and the UV index value shown; user properties such as app language, theme, whether you have premium or are in a trial, and whether the widget is installed; the app version; the device model, operating system and version; and the coarse country or region derived from your IP address. Your coordinates, the names of your saved places and the text of your searches are not sent. The Application does not set a user ID and does not receive or use your advertising identifier (on Android the advertising-ID permission is removed; on iOS the Application does not request tracking permission).
  • Firebase Crashlytics may process, when the Application crashes or reports a non-fatal error: the stack trace and exception details; an installation identifier; the app version and build; the device model, operating system and version, and available memory and storage; the time of the crash; and the technical log leading up to it. It does not capture the contents of your screen.
  • Firebase Remote Config downloads configuration values (such as which onboarding variant to show) and receives the installation identifier and device metadata needed to deliver them.

Availability. Analytics and crash reporting are active in released builds and cannot currently be switched off from inside the Application. They are disabled in development builds. You can stop this processing by uninstalling the Application, you can reset the app-instance identifier by reinstalling it, and you can object to the processing or ask for data associated with your installation to be deleted by writing to dimadyuk.dev@gmail.com. Google processes this data as the Service Provider's processor under the Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms; see Firebase Privacy and Security and the Google Privacy Policy.


Subscriptions and Purchases

Premium features are sold as auto-renewing subscriptions through the Apple App Store or Google Play. Purchases are processed by the store, and managed with the help of RevenueCat. When you make, restore or check a purchase, RevenueCat may process an anonymous App User ID generated by the Application, device and operating-system information, purchase and transaction information, and the receipt or purchase token issued by Apple or Google. RevenueCat does not receive your name or email address from the Application, and the Service Provider never receives your payment card details.


Advertising

The Application does not currently display advertising, does not use the advertising identifier (IDFA / Google Advertising ID) and does not track you across other companies' apps or websites. If advertising is introduced in a future version, this policy will be updated, and any required consent request will be shown, before it is released.


Health and Sun-Safety Information

The Application estimates burn time, sunscreen reapplication timing and vitamin D exposure from your UV level, skin type and settings. These are general-purpose estimates, not medical advice or a medical device. The Application does not read or write Apple Health or Health Connect data and does not collect your age, sex, weight or medical history. The skin-type and sun-exposure choices you make are stored on your device and, as described above, may appear as analytics parameters not linked to your name or account.
